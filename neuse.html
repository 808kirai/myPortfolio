Overview

Neural Sentinel Edge helps rescue teams find survivors in thermal camera footage, even where there is no network. Each field unit runs detection on the spot and sends its results to a command center whenever a connection is available.

Project goal

After earthquakes and floods, or in remote areas, the network is often the first thing to go, and AI tools that depend on the cloud stop working just when rescuers need them. NeuSE keeps detection on the device.

What it does

  • Person detection on thermal drone footage on an ordinary CPU, with a confidence calibrated to mean what it says.
  • Store and forward: detections queue on the unit and go to the command center whenever a link is up, with nothing lost or doubled when it drops.
  • A command center map with every unit, whether it is online and still searching, its backlog, and the ground each camera has already covered.
  • Alerts when an area gets several strong sightings, and plain-language situation reports for coordinators.
  • Safe handling of missing, empty, 16-bit, float and odd-shaped frames, and separate tokens for coordinators and each unit.

Key screens

How it's built

  • Find small warm spots by comparing each pixel with the area around it, so sunlit ground stays dark.
  • Score each spot with a tiny neural network in plain NumPy that looks at its edges, shape and warmth, then fit a person-sized box.
  • Trained on HIT-UAV images in about 3 minutes on a laptop CPU. The weights are 160 KB, and hits scored 0.9 or more were people 92% of the time.
  • A second detector, a small CNN trained with MindSpore and exported to MindIR, runs on the unit with NumPy for comparison.

Where it stands

Next is a MindYOLO person detector trained on Huawei Cloud ModelArts, running on an Orange Pi AIpro with its Ascend NPU.
