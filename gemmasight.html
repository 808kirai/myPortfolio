Overview

Point your phone forward, tap a big button, and GemmaSight says out loud what the camera sees. It reads labels and money, describes what is ahead with hazards first, and guides you to a lost object with clock directions and beeps.

Project goal

Most help for blind people today means asking another person, or using apps that only work in English. GemmaSight runs in any phone browser with no install, speaks English and Kiswahili, and is built on an open-weight model anyone can run.

What it does

  • Read: knows 18 kinds of everyday item and says the key detail first, like the dose and expiry date on medicine or the value of a Kenyan shilling note.
  • Describe: up to two short sentences about what is ahead. Anything that could trip or hurt you comes first, with a warning tone and vibration.
  • Find: say what you are looking for and it keeps checking the camera, giving clock positions like “keys, at 11 o’clock, about 2 steps away”.
  • Ask: speak any question about what the camera sees.
  • One tap switches speech in, speech out and answers to Kiswahili.

Key screens

How it's built

  • The web app opens the rear camera, listens with the browser's speech recognition and talks back with its built-in voice. No audio leaves the phone.
  • On a tap, one frame is shrunk to 768 pixels and sent with the task to a small FastAPI server, which asks Gemma 4 for a small JSON reply.
  • For Find, Gemma only says roughly where the object is. The server turns that into the same clock phrase every time, so directions are never made up.
  • Thinking is turned off on the model call to keep answers fast, and the same prompts can run on a local Gemma in Ollama.

Where it stands

Placed 2nd at an MLH hackathon with me as lead coder. In testing, 15 blindfolded people passed 17 of 21 everyday scenarios.
