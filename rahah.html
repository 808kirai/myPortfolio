Overview

Rahah is a study companion. Students ask about any subject, upload notes, slides or textbook pages, and get answers that point to the page they came from. They then practise with flashcards and tests, and keep track of exams and classes in one place.

Project goal

Build a study tool Kenyan students can afford and trust: answers grounded in their own notes, priced in shillings and paid by M-Pesa, with running costs kept predictable so the price can stay low.

What it does

  • Answers stream in as they are written and cite the page and the quoted passage of the student's own notes.
  • Each question goes to Claude Haiku, Sonnet or Opus depending on how hard it needs to think. Students only see plain names, with Auto as the default.
  • Marks homework from a photo, and turns notes or a past paper into a test with marks, a mark scheme and a model answer for every question.
  • Flashcard decks with spaced repetition: a card comes back after 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 days.
  • Reminders and a timetable for exams, CATs and classes, with phone notifications even when Rahah is closed.
  • A performance page with the student's average, GPA out of 4.0 and scores over time, plus friends and a weekly leaderboard.
  • Pro and Max passes paid by M-Pesa or card through Paystack, with a three-day free trial.

Key screens

How it's built

  • A FastAPI backend on SQLite. Every route checks on the server that the student owns what they ask for.
  • Passwords hashed with scrypt, 30-day sessions in a cookie scripts can't read, and a 15-minute wait after 5 wrong passwords.
  • Long chats send only their later part to Claude, dropped in blocks of 20 messages so prompt caching keeps working and cost stays predictable.
  • Daily limits tracked in money rather than message counts, with a usage and cost box that shows what caching saved.

Where it stands

Of the first 15 students who signed up, 13 (87%) said their CAT scores improved.
